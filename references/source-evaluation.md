# Source Evaluation and Evidence Quality

## Source hierarchy
1. Primary evidence: official product documentation, platform policy, company filings, pricing pages, registry/registrar checks, original datasets, direct customer interviews and first-party product telemetry supplied by the user.
2. High-quality independent evidence: reputable research, established publications, recognized analyst reports, independent testing and credible technical analysis.
3. User-generated evidence: app reviews, Reddit, forums, public social posts, YouTube comments, G2/Capterra/Trustpilot and GitHub issues.
4. Secondary commentary: blogs, affiliate comparisons, SEO pages, press releases and opinion pieces.
5. Unverified claims: anonymous assertions without corroboration.

Do not automatically trust a source because it is official. Official sources are best for what an organization says its policy, feature or price is; independent evidence is often better for real-world performance and customer sentiment.

## Score each source
Assess:
- Authority and provenance.
- Directness (primary vs repeated claim).
- Recency relative to the claim.
- Methodology and sample.
- Independence from other cited sources.
- Incentives or commercial bias.
- Geographic and audience relevance.
- Reproducibility and traceability.

Use High / Medium / Low evidence strength with a short reason. Do not create false numerical precision.

## User reviews and comments
- Treat reviews as qualitative, self-selected evidence.
- Preserve review date, platform, product version and locale when available.
- Cluster themes, remove duplicates and note possible manipulation.
- Separate review volume from review representativeness.
- Do not quote private or sensitive content. Quote public content only when relevant and within applicable platform/copyright rules.
- A single severe report is a signal to investigate, not proof of prevalence.

## Independence
Several sources are not independent if they cite the same press release, syndicated article, dataset or original post. Trace claims to the root source.

## Language
Use “verified” only when directly checked. Use “reported” for user accounts. Use “suggests” for patterns. Use “estimate” for modelled values. Use “hypothesis” for untested explanations. Use “unknown” when evidence is unavailable.
